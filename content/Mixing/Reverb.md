Reverb is a natural acoustic phenomenon. Until very recently, it was an inherent part of the recording process - recording spaces were big and treated acoustically so the recordings contained reverb in them naturally. Today, at its simplest, artificially reverb was made to make recordings sound more natural. **This is how you should think about it most of the time**.


### Parameters
Every reverb event has three parts:

1. Direct signal (the unaffected signal – dry signal)
2. Early reflections – the first bounces, sound hit the first wall and comes back to your ear. **The faster the early reflection, the smaller the room**. Sometimes it’s called size control.
3. Reverb tail / late reflections – all the other echoes and reflections.


**Mix**: If you’re sending to a reverb, make sure the mix is on 100% wet.

**Decay**: RT 60 – how long would it take for the sound to drop 60 dB.

**Pre delay**: zero pre delay make things sound further away, pre delay makes it sound closer. You want it as long as possible without hearing the gap. Pre delay makes the sound more 3 dimensional.

### Tips

- You can use a really fast reverb (less then 300 ml decay) to make sounds sit **behind** other sound. Like a near/far thing.

- You can put a tiny reverb before distortion to make it really gnarly.

- **Create broken instrument:** take a high sine wave and put a crappy reverb and OTT. Bounce and sample as a glitchy instrument. Map a random expression control to the start of the sampler to make it more interesting. **You can do the same with any sound source. We just play the reverb we resampled.**

- Reverb presets are fine. Feel free to tweak them.
