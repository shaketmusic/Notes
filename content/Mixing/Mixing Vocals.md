
Mixing vocals can feel daunting but it's usually a pretty straight forward process. The most important thing is that you get a great performance. That will make your life so much easier. In the process bellow we'll mix a Pop vocal. Other genres may have different tastes for vocal sounds but the process itself is similar.

---

## Part 1 – Editing

Tuning and editing the vocals is the most important thing to do in any mix. Take the time to align and tune the vocals so they are tight. Vocalign is an awesome plugin for that, I do it manually in Melodyne. And **clean up the recording**. No coughing or any noise in between.

## Part 2 – Mixing

### Step 1 – Cleanup EQ
Not making the vocals sound **good,** making them sound **even**

- **Remove rumble (plosives)** – be careful of faze rotation when using filters, it’s going to mess up compression and saturation. Use a faze linear filter instead and render.

- **Deal with resonance** (listen for imbalance and investigate with a spectrum analyzer – the fundamental should be the highest). **Don’t do to many of those** (no more than 2-3)

- **Attaining EQ balance**

- **Distortion?** Use  Decapitator to saturate the vocal a little and make it sound bigger



### Step 2 – Compression

> **Note: whenever your compressor freaks out, it’s probably a frequency issue.**

- **Fix frequency issues** with multiband compression (if you put distortion after your EQ, the multiband should go before it)

- **Compression (1st pass)** **(usually 1176 style - use a precise EQ). Trying to compress the peaks a little (like 3dB)

- **Serial compression (general compressor)** LA2A style - takes longer to react.

**After these steps, listen to a reference track and –**



### Step 3 – genre/context EQ

- **Apply EQ moves** to shape the sounds for the genre. Use reference tracks. This is shaping with big curves, not precise.

- **Use a De-Esser** if needed



### Step 4 – Parallel processing

- **EQ before the reverb** (cut high and low end)
- Think about really small room EQ to make the mic feel like it’s recorded in a bigger studio.
- Delay is usually better for a sense of space so use it. Side chain the delay with the vocals to have it tuck under the vocals
- Optionally send the delay to a big reverb

- **Motown Hype Compression** – parallel compression on the highs (remove the lower frequencies on the parallel heavy compression and mix in) You can add another parallel chain for the lower end and mix a little in. These usually get automated depending on the part of the song. And they are not getting reverb so the reverb goes more to the middle.

 - **Widening** – use micro shift (slightly detune and delay each side from each other) on a parallel track. Mix a little in to make it soind a little bit bigger.




### Step 5 – Doubles

- Don’t hype up the doubles like you did with the lead. Instead, slightly EQ the double – find an EQ where it cuts a little through and boost, cut the low end, a little compression, de esser, a little bit of the bigger reverb. Don’t spend much time on these, it’s about the editing and parts.

- Start wide and tuck it in only if need be.

- To make everything sound cohesive, route everything that’s related to the vocal (vocals and fx) to a new aux track, compress a little and do a smiley face EQ (boost at 60Hz and 8kHz with a wide que) (you can use Waves MV2 and a Pultec EQ)


### Tips

- You should picture the final vocal sound before you start mixing

- Don’t throw plugins, be deliberate and percise

- **Vocal Hack (Good for anything but especially for Rap)**  
1.  Cut the lows and really boost below the filter (at 60hz) with a wide Q. Have a wide boost on the highs (8kHz). So sort of a smiley EQ
2. Heavily compress that
3. Put a multiband limiter for mastering (like a waves L3LL). It’s finding where frequencies get too high, lowering them and making everything else loud.
4. Put a De-Esser
5. That way you get no dynamics but present vocals (Good for Hip Ho and EDM).
