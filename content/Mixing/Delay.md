> Every digital effect except dynamic effects are made of delays – delays (of course), reverbs, echoes, flangers, choruses and even filters.


### Parameters

**Delay**: How much time the delay is. Most delays have sync to notes, dotted notes and triplets. It’s good to add a little offset to make it more “analog”, human.

**Feedback**: Audio goes through the delay, and the feedback circuit lets a small amount of the delayed signal back inside. 100% delay can start to distort on analog style delays.

**Filter/EQ**: It’s a very good idea to filter your delay, especially if it’s digital. Use the filter that’s inside the delay or make a feedback loop so that it gets more filtered every time.

**Modulation**: an LFO that changes the speed of the delays – changes the pitch. **Very** useful when used in moderation. The depth is the amount of modulation and the rate is the frequency of the lfo. You can use a really short delay to create vibrato.

### Delay types

Short, Medium, Long.

- **Long delay** is everything that’s greater than 50 ms – we hear **a discreate echo**. The more percussive the sound, the faster we can go.

- **Short delay** is less then 20 ms – we hear **a timbre change** because of the phase relationships. We can make a comb filter that way.  If we add feedback we can create a flanger.

- **Medium Delay** is 20 to 50 ms Is good for fake doubling (classic pop tracks have 5 doubles on the lead vocals, side note).


### Tips

- To make a stereo delay, slightly offset the two delays from each other.

- **Haas effect delay** – one side is being offset a little bit (hard panned left and right), the delay is fully wet to not have anything in the middle.

- **Slap Echo** – a replacement for reverb. The time is 80 – 200 ms, no feedback, filtering and modulation. sounds vintage, vibe, spooky.

- **Tempo Synced delay 1** – Delay is really good for spicing up the sound rhythmically. Don’t put the delay lines at the same tempo as the dry signal.

- **Tempo Synced delay 2** – you can put a delay on longer notes to make some more interesting.

- Use modulation (Chorus, flanger, ensemble) to blur sounds that are two in focus (we can’t have everything in focus, we need to have our focal point)
