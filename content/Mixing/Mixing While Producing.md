
### Distortion as sound shaper

Distortion is anything that changes the waveform and adds different harmonics to the sound

**Harmonics** – integer multiples of the fundamental

Back in the day (before 1999) every recording and mixing process had multiple points of harmonic distortion (the mic, the pre, the console) because of the analog gear. Digital gear doesn’t have that kind of distortion, and it can make it sound boring. So we need harmonic distortion to give the production life.

> Instead of getting harmonics from reach waveforms like saw’s and squares we can distort sine waves. When the notes overlap into the distortion we get different sets of harmonics. Distortion makes the sound more interactive. They will respond to velocity in more interesting ways, too. See [[Distortion]].

### The Mixing Elements in a production mindset

- Try to produce in a way that doesn’t make mixing harder (like make sure even if your bass  has fazing that the fundamental isn’t with wave editing/ multiband dynamics/having a sub and harmonics differently. See [[Unison]].

- When picking drum sounds, put MIDI first and check them in context.

- Don’t layer because you don’t like the sound, layer with intent. Think about how to make it more **Tall, Deep, Wide** (frequency, Space, Panning). Adding some space, more frequency content, widening the sound with Ableton’s align delay (the HAAS effect). It’s about seeing what’s missing.

- Think about the headroom. Clip sounds if necessary to remove unnecessary spikes.

- Use Ableton’s **extract loop** to get perfect timing of your drums and loops for sidechain and in general

- **Bus Compression/Glue Compression**: Put compression on the whole drum bus to have everything more glued together

- **Parallel Compression**: typically use a send. It’s combining a REALLY compressed sound with not compressed sound, we mix a little of the compressed signal in. Any time we want to inflate or bring out more detail, to have more sustain or have them sound more full we can use parallel compression.

- Limit your drum group just enough to eliminate the picks but not enough to hear it. A lot of electronic production is about micro mastering in the mixing stage.

- Make sounds and Use EQ to feel specific frequency ranges that you’re missing.

- **Subtractive arrangement** – we know how the most full loop sounds like. Now we start subtracting layers for different sections and build an arrangement. It’s a great way to write quickly.

- **Prepping for mastering** – slightly limit everything. Try clipping everything except the drums. And side chain everything to the kick (with a fast release so it’s not as noticeable). Then limit again the whole thing. The vocals shouldn’t be limited until the last limiter on the master track.
