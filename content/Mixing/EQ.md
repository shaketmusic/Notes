Equalization is the process of adjusting the balance (the levels) between frequences of an audio signal.

**Types of Eq’s**
1. **Graphic EQ’s** – Let you control only the gain of the bends. The bend frequencies and curves are set. Usually are being used as "Flavor" EQ's, to add a certain character to the sound. 
2. **Semi Parametric** – Frequency is adjustable, but the Q is not. (API consoles have semi parametric EQ)
3.  **Parametric EQ’s** – you can change every parameter (frequency and Q).

**Band** – a specific frequency that can be processed

**Frequency** – what frequency of the sound am I manipulating? Because the scale is exponential, the difference between 60-65 hertz is a lot more dramatic than 4000-4005 hertz.

**Gain** – the gain of the curve. Are we boosting or cutting?

**Bandwidth (Q)** – the width of the band, scaled exponentially (to keep the same ratio). A Q of 1 = 1 octave. Q of 12 – 1/12 of an octave (one piano key).

**Filters** – remove frequency above, bellow, between or around a center point (High pass, low pass, Band-pass, Bend reject/notch).


### Why we EQ

1. **Creative** – use for sound design (accent certain frequencies, transform the sound). **Not for mixing**.

2. **Corrective** – fixing subjective problems (what’s wrong with the source?). getting read of rumble, room resonances, the wrong microphone. Not thinking about the song but only about the source itself. **Corrective EQ should be minimal**

3. **Blending/context EQ**. Making one sound worse to make the whole mix sound better. Sacrifice the guitar for the sake of the vocal. Choose between the kick and the base. How do we make the sounds fit together?

> Unless it’s a specific objective issue, **Don’t EQ in solo**. You need to hear it in context. You can solo two tracks that are fighting, though.
