
### general
**Where to start?**
We start building the song from the foundation, starting with the most important instrument and going from there.

**Do I need to fix this track?**
Ask yourself – does this track work in the frequency? Dynamics? Space?

**Good Enough**
Make the best decision with the information you have – we’re building the track, adding tracks one by one, so if it sounds good enough, let’s continue and get back later if need be. Is there a reason to compress this kick with what we have right now?



### Starting Out

- **We start from the section where most of the tracks play, including the lead vocal**. We first go to a dense section and go backwards.

- **Think about Balance of every instrument** Does it lack mids? Highs? Lows? Does it has rumble?

- **Clicky kicks are good especially for EDM**. It helps the, kick to cut in the mix no matter the system

- **Start with your ears, then use an analyzer**. Investigate what sounds good and look at the analyzer too see if you’re in the ball park. But your ears should lead the decisions.

### Dynamics control

- **Think about the headroom as your budget**. It is limited, so every unnecessary pick should be cut. Some sounds have unnecessary picks – clip them/limit them to create headroom.

- **Compressor – Limiter – Clipper**. Sometimes compressors are not enough, use limiting or clipping. Clipping will make the sound a little brighter because it turns the sine waves into squares. Don’t clip too much! Saturators take the picks down and inflate the quite part.

- **Try all possibilities and pick what you liked the most**. Don’t go into a song determining in advance what you’re going to do and commit to it.

- **Generally, you want your snare to stop before the next kick starts.** Think about that when applying reverb to the snare. When producing, time your snare to fill the gaps between the kicks.

- **Sometimes dynamics issues are actually frequency issues**. Listen and look for picks. The compressor shouldn’t jump between -12 and -3.

- **Dynamic frequency problems** – when some words sound good, some don’t. Use multiband compression or dynamic EQ.

- **Serial Compression** – one compressor catches the big dynamics (so a high ratio that triggers almost only the high picks) and another compressor with a low ratio and low threshold.


### Space

- **Reverb/Slap Echo**: if we don’t have room for reverb, or want vibe/vintage, go for slap echo. You can also do a slap echo to a plate reverb.
- You can use a mono reverb to keep sounds in their pan, instead of a stereo reverb that would pull it back to the middle.

- Another trick (especially for lead guitars and slide guitar) is to pan the dry sound and the reverb to opposite sides.


### Putting it Together

-  If the Foundation and Focal point sound good, everything else is pretty easy to put into place.

- **Now we try to put the other tracks in, find their place. From the most important to the least.** We look to see how much low end can we remove before it starts to sound bad. **Never do this in solo.** Then lower the whole eq spectrum and divide the spectrum to ten octaves: 31, 63, 125, 250, 500, 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k, 16k. Then we try to find a boost where the sound cuts through the mix.

- Don’t boost where you already boosted. You can boost one sound where you already boosted.
