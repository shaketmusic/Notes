
I'm not really a mastering engineer, and I would advise going to one if you have the budget. However, it's good to know how to do some mastering yourself so that you can have a sense of how the mix will sound mastered.

I do my own mastering, but I don't feel qualified to teach you about it. However, here are some tips.


- Your mix should sound really good even before mastering. The less you need to do in Mastering the better. My masters are mostly just making the song louder.

- Use multiband compression if you need more punch from the kicks. Find the attack of the kick (slow attack fast release) and compress the rest of the lows. Use this when the 808 is louder than the kick.

- Use Sooth or Gulfoss to suppress resonances.

- Use loudness meters to check your overall level. Professional music is between -10 to -4 LUFS. **Don’t master for -14 LUFS.**

- Putting a clipper on the master is cool, but sometimes it can make some frequencies sound harsh, because we hear them for too long. I use dynamic EQ to help with that.

- if you're mastering yourself and a certain track doesn't work, just go back to fix it in the mix.
