A handy checklist for everything you should do when you start a mixing project.


1. Use the sample rate that was used for the recording. The bit rate wouldn’t change the recorded files, only new recordings (so not really relevant for mixing)

2. Import all audio. In Ableton, if you hold option and drug the files, they will appear on individual tracks. **Make sure to collect all and save in Ableton to make sure it will copy the files to the project folder**. If you hold option and drug the files in ProTools, it will actually copy the files to the project folder.

3. Name and color code your tracks (it’s good to always use the same colors for the same types - drums always red, vocals always purple etc.).

4. **If you use ProTools** - Create a master fader (shift command n opens a new track. Hold shift cmnd and down arrow to open a new kind of track). Maybe put all your vocals in the right of the master and the instrumental left to the master

5. Put markers for the song sections (function return).

6. **Set the Tempo**. Use the tap tempo (press t after you click the tempo) and find what tempo we're in so everything lines up to the beat.

7. Gain staging. The agreed SOL for digital audio is -18dB. **Reduce the gain of really loud sound**s. NOT THE VOLUME FADER. You can use a monitor plugin set to -18dB.

8. **Phase Checks** – Whenever you have the same sound captured by multiple mics, like drums, piano, guitar - you could have phase issues. They are the most fundamental issues. Use the TRIM plugin (ProTools’ utility). Look for snare top/bottom, Overheads, kick in/out and compare to overheads, room. Always compare to the overheads.

### Tips
- **In an electronic production, you could have low end phase issues in similar sounds even if it’s not the same source (like drum loops to kicks or two different kicks or snares)**.

- Be careful of filtering or EQing all the lows, that could change the phase.

- **Bellow 100 Hz have only 1 kick and 1 bass**
