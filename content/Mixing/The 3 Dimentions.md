Picture your mix as a shoe box. Every song, every mix is the exact same size (our **headroom**) and we try to fill it with different elements.

Every 3D space has, well three Dimentions. In relation to music, these are:

- **Tall –** frequency. The way we use the frequency range.
- **Deep** – sense of near and far (using volume, reverb/delay, lowpass/high pass)
- **Wide**  - left/right, panning

By contrasting the "bigness" of the track at curtain points we create tension and interest. Not only having your mix tall, but manipulating the height as the song progresses. Setting expectation and delivering. In the song Teenage Dream (Katy Perry) the bass (spectrum wise) comes in only in the chorus. The same thing can be done with width (in rock the chorus is wide – they double the guitars and vocals).

So it’s about **how to sound full, add tension without being boring**. Think about that when producing, and when mixing. Hight, width, depth. If the chorus is not hitting, let’s slightly remove some low-end and high-end 8 bars before. Let’s slightly narrow it. And then bring everything back for the chorus. Electronic producers do that all the time, but you'd be surprised how many times you hear it even in Folk.
