There are five roles/elements that need to happen in a typical pop (as in popular music, not the genre itself) mix. Each of the elements in the song needs to serve one element or it doesn’t fit. This is also the order to mix.

We start from complete silence, treat the foundation and move on to the focal point, rhythmic elements etc. Instead of starting with everything, we begin with a single track and build from there.

### Foundation 
Whatever in the production that holds it together – kick, bass, snare, main riff. The when mixing the foundation, it’s going to be full frequency-wise. The foundation needs to have full frequency and most of the times be really compressed. **The foundation rarely changes**.

### Focal Point 
The melody. The vocals, the main instruments. Essentially – what the listener is supposed to listen too. What do we pay attention to? Most of the time mixes don’t have a lack of focal point, but a focal point that's not clear enough. There might be too many focal points that are fighting for our attention.

### Rhythmic Interest
Once we are past mixing the Foundation and Focal Point, everything else needs to support these. We add some rhythmic loops that are quite, small frequency wise that are supporting the foundation

### Pads
if the mix we’re making is a soup, pads are the broth. It glues everything, gives sense of space. It doesn’t need to be actually a pad (it could be a space guitar, or vocals). Pads fill in the gaps and sit around the music.

### Supporting actors/Ear candy 
Extra things that make the listener go “ooh”. Little things to put where nothing happens that prevent the song from being boring. That way every moment has a thing in it. All the miscellanies stuff. We can add ear candies if we feel a moment needs something in the mixing stage. Drum fills, sweeps, risers etc.
