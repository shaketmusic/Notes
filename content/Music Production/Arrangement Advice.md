Arrangement, in my experience, is often one of the most obvious differences between starting and experienced producers. A good arrangement will make your mixes easier and better. It will make your drums groove. It will make your song feel alive and interesting without you working harder.

- Listen to records and try to analyze the arrangement: Which instrument is playing and where? How does the overall energy arc flow? What are the instrument parts doing and how do they relate to each other? (call and response? working together?) 

- For new drum grooves inspiration, I especially recommend listening to traditional African and South American music. 

- Learn voice leading, counterpoint basics

- Listen to your inspirations' inspirations. 


### Tips

- Good songs don’t have things repeat exactly the same. There’s always something changing.

- How many chord progression repeat really affects how long a song feel.

- Try to write four-five part harmonies instead of chords.

- To make ambient – process something, put reverb, bounce, stretch a lot with texture warp, add more reverb.

- Steal ideas from different genres and bring them to your own. A drum groove, a sound design trick, an aesthetic... This is how new sounds and genres are born - from different and unique influences. 

- Put cool sound snippets in a drum rack, have them all on the same choke group and play. Really good for live drops, especially dubstep.

- Use silence. Even mute the entire track
