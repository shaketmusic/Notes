Vocal chops are a great way to have an instrumental section with the appeal of having vocals (for non-musicians who love pop).

The first vocal is probably The Singing Dogs – Jingle Bells from 1955. It was done with cutting and speeding up and down tape. When it came out, people thought it was real.

**There are 4 ways to do vocal chops**

1. **Audio**
Find a slice, copy it and paste it. Don’t forget you can use SHIFT + OPTION to scrub through the sample.

2. **Melody**
Grab a relatively long part of the vocals, drug to sampler and play a melody. This is the old school way.

3. **Sampler**
We use splice mode in simpler, with warp on. If the vocal is rhythmic we chop it by beats. Make sure you go to gate mode. Use through playback mode

  ![[Vocal Chops With a Sampler.png]]

4. **Drum Rack**
We through the whole vocals to drum rack. Gate mode, warp on. Find a cool snippet, tune it. Copy to the next one, move the playback flag and find another spot, tune, etc.

![[Vocal Chops with Drum Rack.png]]

### Advice

1. **Come up with the rhythm first.** Even if you’re not sure about the melody, just take one chop and put the rhythm first.

2. **Process first**. It’s easier to work when the sound source sounds good. So, before you even write a melody, process the vocals:

	1. Tune the vocals
	2. We don’t want any dynamics. Horribly compress using peak mode and auto makeup, pull the threshold down.
	3. Saturate to the point where the vocals break, then mix a tiny bit (like 10%)
	4. EQ: boost above 1k to have more presence. Cut the rumble
	5. Add a little bit of reverb, to taste.
	6. OTT. _Take two utilities, group together and map their gain to the same macro. Invert one of them and put the OTT in the middle._


3. **Use a combination of the methods.** Work with a method until you want something else, then do another layer with a different method. As long as it’s all the same process it will sound as one track.

4. **Selective layering**. A borrow classical music arranges. From Instead of layering the entire melody, layer a few accent notes. Change the octave, do the haas effect with align delay

5. **Vocode the vocal chops**. You can mix 18% of the vocoder, or even full. Maybe even vocode your melody?

6. **Automate the reverb**. Mute the vocal chop with the reverb in every place where you have breaks in the melody.
