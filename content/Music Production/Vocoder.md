The filter band Vocoder:

![[Vocoder Overview.png]]


One signal is a carrier and the second is the modulator – modulates the carrier. The modulator goes into the filter bank and generates an envelope follower for each filter band. The carrier is going through an identical filter bank, controlled by the same envelope follower.

>**If the modulator is brighter** – **the carrier will be brighter. But we never hear the modulator.**

- In Ableton, put the vocoder on the modulator. From that point we’re not going to hear it anymore.

- For a good sounding vocoder – use a harmonically reach, almost noisy, volume coherent source. Like a saw, square, supersaw, unison saw/square etc.

- To make a sound more accurate, adjust the range to the range the source is actually using.

- When you turn the bandwidth down, there are gaps in the bends that gives a watery/glassy sound. Some bass music producers are using this effect. You can also make them overlap.

- The **gate** is a spectral gate – works on each band independently.

- Take a vocal – put through vocoder – use BW and Gate to make it ring notes – add reverb – stretch – add more reverb = ambient.

- The **release** sounds kind of like a harmonic reverb, blurring the ends. Can be a good alternative to reverb.

- The **attack** will get rid of the spottiness/noisiness. It fades everything in.

- **Formant** is the natural EQ curve a an instrument.

- **Depth** determines what will the vocoder track – with it all the way down tracking only the rhythm and not the words.

- **Mono** will mono both carrier and modulator.

- **Stereo** the carrier is stereo but the modulator is mono

- **L/R** stereo carrier and stereo modulator. Better for cool sound design stuff.

- **Enhance** helps with bad carriers**.** It tries to normalize the carrier. Most times it’s best to just pick a better carrier.

- **Unvoiced** adds some noise on the s’ to add intelligibility.
