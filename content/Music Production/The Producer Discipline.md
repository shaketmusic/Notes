> *“When I went into the studio I created a sound that I wanted to hear.” - Phil Spector*



What is the role of a music producer? Sound designer? Arranger? Coach? 
All of the above and more, of course. But also non of the above. In the 10 years that I've been producing, I've come to learn that a music producer's job is simple understand, yet hard to achieve - **To understand the idea driving the song, and realize it into reality**.



### General Advice for any producer

- **Dedicate time every week to sound design presets**
	We all want inspiring and different sounds that will just work in a production, but nothing hurts the flow of a session more then a producer that takes 30 minutes to design a new sound.

- **Dedicate time every week to analyze songs**
	You could argue that a producer's best tool is his/her ears. We can actively use them to learn how songs and productions work, what makes them great or where they fail. How would we know which choice to make if we don't analyze and understand what already works?

- **There are no bad choices. Not really** 
	Every choice just impacts the decisions you make later. You can make decisions that don't fit, but that's not because their bad. They just don't match well together. So faced with a dilemma - just decide and let your decision lead the process and the later decisions.

- **Sometimes it’s best to start over**
	if the idea is good but the truck is not working for some reason

- **Music Production is like cooking**
	Like cooking, to get depth of sound design use multiple processes. Chop, cook, bake, fry - Bounce, Process, sample, Bounce again….



### Analyzing

- Reference tracks are really useful when you try to analyze why your track doesn’t work. 

- Look for why the reference track works and what we’re working on not. It could be the rhythm, groove, arrangement, form. 

- A lot of the time what makes the songs good are musical more then technical aspects. Analyze the songs in the genre you’re producing to see what musically they do different than you


### Analysis Tools

Our ears are invaluable, but they can also lie, be imprecise or get used to certain things. That is why we need to use other methods, in addition. Listen with your ears, confirm with your eyes.

**Free analysis tools you should use** (I'm not sponsored, this is what I use)

- [Span](https://www.voxengo.com/product/span/) - A spectrum analyzer

- [MOscilloscpe](https://www.meldaproduction.com/MOscilloscope) - An Oscilloscpe. Also look at [Scyllascope](https://dsgdnb.com/plugins/scyllascope)

- Use a **View track** (a resampled track of the master that you record to and inspect)
