### Micro modulation (per note) vs Macro modulation (Performance)
When designing sounds, we first think about the **micro modulation** (how the sounds changes on a small scale from moment to moment) and then about the **macro modulation** (How a sound changes from section to section) in context of the song.

- Random modulation. Random pitch/cuttof make sounds sound analog and alive. Random modulation could be used to make the sounds feel alive.

- **Variation is key for interesting tracks.** While often it is the result of arrangements or part changes, it can come from modulation of the sounds themselves, too.

### Envelopes
**Attack** – the time from initial to pick
**Decay** – the time from pick to sustain
**Sustain** – the level in which volume stays
**Release** - the time it takes from release of the key to complete silence

### Less sources, more destinations

When it comes to time-locked modulation, professional sounds have one performance gestures (like an LFO) controlling a lot of parameters

*“Use the wavetable to FM the second oscillator. Apply a shark fin LFO to Volume, Drive amount, Drive Mix, High EQ shelf gain.”*

You can add another, second LFO half the speed (quarters and halves for example). Modulate a different thing with it. Now you get two variations.

### Modulating Modulators

When we think about modulation, we usually think about a modulator modulation an element of the sound itself, like the filter, the volume or the pitch. But in most modern synths almost any parameter can be modulated, including the modulators themselves.

- If we have an LFO modulating pitch, we can assign a random modulation to its frequency, to get a more natural, changing vibrato

- For pluck sounds, modulating envelope decay and release times can create very dynamic and musical sounds, even if we have a very simple synth voice.

- Lets say we have a pad. If we use an envelope with a slow attack to control the mod depth of an LFO controlling the filter, we get a smooth sound that gradually becomes modulated.

- Arpeggiators, LFO rates, Envelope stages times, Modulation amounts are all great modulation destinations.
