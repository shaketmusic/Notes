
Try to choose your drums in context. Only then you can understand if they sound good. **Sometimes, because we start production with drums, it’s a good idea to replace the drums after the production is done.**

- You don’t need to check out all the options, if a sound is good you can move on.

- Reuse sounds you like

- Your Kick pattern needs to have some variation: AAAB, ABAC, AABA…

- **Random modulation** on pitch, filter and decay to create variations

- **It’s cool to sometimes add a reverse element before a snare**

- Add a pitched, in key element. Can be even a sine with a fast pitch envelope and reverb. Make sure it’s one note and not a melody.

### Big kick/Small Kick
With busier kick patterns, make the secondary hits softer, quitter, using a different kick. It’s like having ghost notes.

Put the kick in a drum rack, duplicate the kick, make it a bit quieter, lower in pitch and duller (using an EQ). Shorten it also a bit.

### Main Drums and Secondary drums
If you’re putting percussion, make it very clear which are the main drums and which are the secondary. Filter them a bit, lower their volume, and put a tiny bit of reverb to make them further back.

### Reverbs
Instead of putting reverb on the snare, put another sound with reverb fully wet and layer it with the snare. That way we get the clarity of the snare and the ambience of the reverb.

### Foley Sounds
A lot of electronic drums lack texture.

Record random, sparse foley hits you make yourself (it can be anything – hitting the table, clapping, bottles, etc.). Grab anything that looks like a transient and move it to the grid (loose quantization). Pitch Up, Consolidate, put warp mode to one shot and shorten it. Consolidate again and quantize with SHIFT+CMND+U.

You can try to put warp to texture with high flux, long duration. Consolidate and Quantize.

-       You can layer foley with major hits like snare hits.

-       You can ad texture “loops” in the background

### Fractal Phrasing
While the main elements repeat, we add small elements that add variation, like a slightly different Hi Hat. Now instead of a small repeating pattern, we get a long non-repeating pattern, just from this one change.

**Micro timing** – most of the song don’t use it, but it’s still really cool. You can steal groove (like from Dangelo voodoo in Ableton)

### Tuning the Drums
A **pitch shifter** maintains the frequency content and relations of the sound - multiplication. A **frequency shifter** will move every frequency by the same amount - addition. When you pitch something, the higher frequencies move a lot less than the lows (preception-wise), so when we want to tune drums, it’s a better idea to use a frequency to preserve the character and tone of the drum.
