# Power Source

- Never connect any of the circuits nor experiment with a high-voltage (e.g., “mains”, household) power supply/source.
- Only work with USB power supplies and/or low voltage batteries.
- Always disconnect USB power connection and/or batteries before working on a circuit and double-check the circuit before applying USB or battery power.
- Always disconnect USB power connection and/or batteries after you’re done experimenting with the circuit.
- Do not close a circuit without putting one or more components in between the positive and negative terminals of a power supply. These components will restrict the current flow, slowing down the current. Without these components the current will try to be infinite. Your power supply can't provide infinite current, but it will provide as much as it can. This could cause your wire to burn up, damage the power supply, drain your battery, or worse. Most of the time your power supply will have some sort of safety mechanism built into it to limit the maximum current in the event of a short circuit, but not always.
- If you notice that things are suddenly becoming hot or a part suddenly burns out, immediately turn off the power and look for possible short circuits.

# General Tool Handling

- Always wear and use appropriate safety equipment, e.g., eye protection, ventilation, gloves.
    - Always use eye protection when using power tools.
    - Ensure that any soldering, painting, or gluing is done in a well ventilated room.
    - Consider wearing a mask to prevent inhaling dust or fumes.
- Never wear loose-fitting garments or jewelry when using power tools, as they might get caught in the tools.

# Soldering

- Wash your hands after a soldering session to clean any leftover lead.
    - You may use disposable gloves for long soldering sessions or if you prefer not to touch solder altogether.
- Do not inhale the fumes that occur when burning solder. The rosin core flux can be harmful and cause respiratory deceases, e.g., asthma.
- Never leave a hot soldering iron unattended.
- Always turn off or disconnect soldering irons and other tools after use.
- Use a sturdy table when soldering and avoid placing the soldering iron near flammable materials.
- Never grab for a soldering iron if you drop it. Pick it up as soon as it settles on the table or floor.

# Glue Gun

- Be mindful of heat and be careful with the nozzle to avoid burns.
- Use the glue gun’s stand when not in use.
- Never leave a hot glue gun unattended.
- Always turn off or disconnect glue guns after use.
- Make sure the circuit is disconnected before gluing components together.
- Have a piece of paper underneath the glueing components to avoid damaging your working surface.

# Drill / Dremel

- Wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from flying debris.
- Use a clamp or a Panavise to avoid slippage.
- Hold the drill firmly and make sure your hands are clear from the drill bit.

# Prototyping and Protecting Sensitive Electronic Components

- Avoid testing with expensive microphones, headphones, amps, keyboards, and other equipment to prevent accidentally damaging it.
- Keep sensitive components in the antistatic pouch or foam they were shipped in.
- Touch a metal object to discharge static electricity prior to handling sensitive components.
- Consider using an antistatic wrist strap and/or mat when working with sensitive components.
- Double check the orientation of components in a circuit before applying power.
- Use copper alligator clips as a heat sink when soldering temperature sensitive components.
